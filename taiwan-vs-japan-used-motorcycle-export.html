Taiwan vs Japan Used Motorcycle Export: Which Fits Your Market?

Most wholesale buyers researching used motorcycle sourcing compare these two markets at some point. Neither is universally "better" — they tend to fit different parts of a buyer's product mix. Here's how they actually differ.

Why buyers choose Taiwan

Taiwan requires mandatory annual vehicle inspection and has one of the highest scooter-ownership rates in the world, producing a large fleet of small-displacement scooters with traceable service history. For markets where the demand is everyday commuter transport rather than larger-displacement bikes, Taiwan's fleet composition and lower average price point are usually the better fit — see the Used Motorcycle Supplier for Africa guide for how this plays out in Luckwell's current markets.

Where Japan tends to fit better

Japan's export trade covers a broader range of displacements and model types, sourced largely through established auction systems with their own standardized grading. Buyers building out a product line that includes larger-displacement or more specialized models often look to Japan for that segment, sometimes alongside a Taiwan supplier covering the small-displacement, higher-volume end of the same market.

Many established wholesale buyers don't pick one country exclusively — they source everyday commuter scooters from Taiwan and larger or specialty models from Japan, matching each source to the segment it's strongest in.

What to check regardless of source country

  • Who performs the inspection and grading, and do you receive photos before final payment — see the Pre-Shipment Inspection Checklist.
  • What the actual MOQ is, and whether a smaller entry-level order is available.
  • Whether the exporter ships directly from their own inventory or is a reseller/broker.

Frequently asked questions

What's the main difference between Taiwan and Japan as used motorcycle export sources?

Taiwan's fleet is dominated by small-displacement scooters (50cc–180cc) sold directly by exporters at a fixed wholesale MOQ. Japan's export fleet includes a wider mix of displacements and is typically sourced through auction houses using Japan's own condition-grading system, often at higher average price points.

Which source is better for African and Middle Eastern buyers?

It depends on the target vehicle type. Buyers looking for everyday small-displacement scooters at a lower price point per unit typically favor Taiwan; buyers looking for larger-displacement or specialty models often look to Japan, sometimes sourcing from both to cover different segments of their local market.

Is Taiwan's grading system the same as Japan's?

No. Taiwan exporters like Luckwell use an A/B/C grading standard set by the individual exporter's own pre-shipment inspection. Japan's export trade commonly references auction-house or inspection-body grading scales, which are standardized across many Japanese exporters rather than set by one company.

Does Taiwan or Japan have a lower MOQ for a first order?

Taiwan wholesale exporters like Luckwell offer a 50-unit 20FT container option specifically to lower the entry point for first-time or smaller buyers, alongside the standard 125-unit 40HQ order.
